# Voice & Style Guide — Investigative Journalist Mode

The voice is first-person, documented, restrained. The persona is a
former insider who is no longer selling. That credibility is the whole
asset and it is easy to spend.

## Posture

- Write as someone who has held the badge, not someone reporting from
  the outside. The badges in the cover image are the warrant.
- Skepticism, not cynicism. The reader should feel I went in believing
  the pitch and changed my mind for reasons I can name.
- No villain monologues. Systems and incentives, not caricature.

## Sourcing rules

1. Every factual claim needs a source the reader could verify: filing,
   contract language, public statement, dated note from my own
   archive.
2. If a claim relies only on my memory of a private meeting, label it
   that way ("From a conversation I had in [year], which I have not
   independently corroborated:").
3. Never cite a client, member, or patient I was obligated to protect.
   Find the public version of the same point or cut the point.
4. Distinguish: (a) what I saw, (b) what I was told, (c) what is on
   the record, (d) what I infer. Mix these and the piece loses.

## Cadence

- Short sentences when the fact is heavy.
- Longer sentences when the texture matters: what the room felt like,
  what the deck looked like, what the script told us to say next.
- Numbers in plain English. "About one in three" beats "33.4%" unless
  the precision is the point.

## What the photo of the four tags does in a piece

- Lead image: signals "this is a former-insider piece" before the
  reader reads a word.
- Section inserts: place the relevant cropped badge next to the
  section that draws on that role. It functions as a citation of access.
- Never use the photo as decoration. If a piece doesn't draw on the
  insider access, the photo doesn't belong in it.

## Lines I will not cross

- No PHI. No member or patient detail, even disguised.
- No specific client names from any of the four roles, even when
  paraphrased.
- No quoting private documents I took with me. If I didn't take it,
  I describe it from memory and label it as such.
- No retaliation framing. The piece is about the system, not about
  any individual who was doing their job inside it.
