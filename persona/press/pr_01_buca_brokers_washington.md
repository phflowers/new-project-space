# As BUCA Carriers and Big-Box Brokers Roll Out 2027 Renewals, Who Is Checking the Math in Washington?

## Fiduciary Advisor Paul H. Flowers Jr. calls the current renewal cycle "a field day" and urges CFOs to demand independent claims audits before signing.

**OAK BROOK, IL — [DATE], 2026 —** As Blue Cross, UnitedHealthcare, Cigna and Aetna — collectively known inside the employee-benefits industry as the "BUCA" carriers — send 2027 renewal quotes to America's employers, and as the largest consolidator brokerages pass those quotes through to their clients with limited resistance, longtime benefits insider Paul H. Flowers Jr. is publicly challenging the process and the vacuum of federal oversight surrounding it.

"The industry has a name for what this quarter looks like," said Flowers, CEO of Superior Insurance Advisors and a partner in the NextGen Benefits Mastermind Partnership. "In the room, we call it a field day. Some independent forecasts put employer premium increases for 2027 as high as 18%. Nine-and-a-half percent is the polite floor. Meanwhile, the offices in Washington that are supposed to be examining what's driving those numbers are either understaffed, out of scope, or waiting for the next administration to tell them what their scope is."

Flowers' comments arrive against a backdrop of federal enforcement activity that, in his view, tells employers exactly how little cover they can expect from Washington on this renewal:

- The **Federal Trade Commission** secured a landmark settlement with Express Scripts on February 4, 2026, requiring the pharmacy benefit manager to adopt fundamental changes to its business practices — with parallel proceedings against Caremark and OptumRx, the other two of the "Big Three" PBMs, still in negotiation. *(Source: FTC press release, 2026-02-04.)*
- The **MultiPlan multidistrict litigation (MDL 3121)** cleared the way in February 2026 for depositions of MultiPlan representatives on allegations that the company's algorithms facilitated more than $19 billion in single-year out-of-network underpayments to providers. **Aetna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Cigna, Elevance, and UnitedHealthcare** are named as co-defendants. *(Source: MDL 3121 case management order, 2026-02-27.)*
- **Kraft Heinz** has filed an ERISA action against Aetna alleging $1.3 million in claims paid without required documentation and refusal to share plan data with the employer. *(Source: Kraft Heinz Foods Co. v. Aetna Life Ins. Co., federal district court complaint.)*
- The **Independent BROKERS TIME Act of 2025 (S.2625)** was introduced in the U.S. Senate to strengthen requirements for independent broker conduct — but has not advanced out of committee. *(Source: Congress.gov, 119th Congress.)*

"None of these cases were brought by Congress," Flowers said. "They were brought by employers, providers, and one federal agency doing the job the other agencies aren't. If you're a CFO or an HR leader looking at a BUCA quote this month, that should tell you exactly how much external oversight you can count on before you sign."

Flowers, who held roles at four major benefits and healthcare organizations — USI Insurance Services, Cigna, Hines & Associates, and Fountain Health — before earning his fiduciary designation, argues that the U.S. mid-market is now openly two-tier. Employers with an independent, fiduciary advisor, he says, are quietly renegotiating PBM contracts, exercising claims-level audit rights, and moving toward properly structured self-funded or level-funded plans. Employers still working through consolidator broker channels, in his phrasing, "are signing what they're handed."

He points to the May 2026 Jones Day industry note titled *Rising Scrutiny of Employer Health Plan Administration* as evidence that the wave of ERISA fiduciary-breach litigation aimed at employer plan sponsors is only accelerating.

The **NextGen Benefits Mastermind Partnership**, of which Flowers is a partner, has published for more than a decade an annual savings benchmark of **at least $2,000 per employee per year** returned to employer balance sheets when plans are moved to a fiduciary-advised structure — a benchmark Flowers argues is routinely available to any employer willing to open its plan data to an independent reviewer before its next renewal window closes.

"Washington is not coming to save this renewal," Flowers said. "Your fiduciary duty as an HR leader or CFO does not require Washington's permission. It requires you to look."
