# You Wouldn't Let a Convicted Predator Near Your Children. So Why Do HR Leaders Still Hand Adjudicated Bad Actors the Keys to Their Employees' Healthcare?

## A fiduciary benefits advisor argues that the standard American parents apply to who watches their children is the same standard ERISA already requires of plan sponsors selecting benefit vendors — and, on the public court record, is not the standard those sponsors are applying.

**OAK BROOK, IL — [DATE], 2026 —** Fiduciary benefits advisor Paul H. Flowers Jr., one of the first ten Validation Institute–certified Fiduciary Advisors in the United States, is issuing a direct challenge to human resources professionals and chief financial officers on what he calls "the most quietly damaging double standard in corporate America."

"No responsible parent in this country would let Jeffrey Epstein or anyone from his documented network babysit their child," Flowers said. "The reason is simple: the public court record makes that person unsafe to trust with something precious. What I want to ask HR leaders and CFOs — plainly — is why we accept a lower standard when it comes to who we hand our employees' families over to at open enrollment."

Flowers, CEO of Superior Insurance Advisors and a partner in the NextGen Benefits Mastermind Partnership, is explicit that his framing is an **analogy about fiduciary judgment** — not a comparison of any specific company or executive to any criminal defendant. The point, he says, is the *standard of care*: the same standard American families apply to protecting their children is exactly the standard ERISA already requires employer plan sponsors to apply when selecting benefit vendors — and the standard many are failing to apply.

The public court record he references is not conjecture:

- The **Federal Trade Commission** filed action against the three largest U.S. pharmacy benefit managers — Caremark, Express Scripts, and OptumRx — alleging that their rebating practices artificially inflated the list price of insulin drugs sold to American patients. On February 4, 2026, the FTC secured a landmark settlement with Express Scripts requiring fundamental changes to its business practices, projected to lower patient out-of-pocket costs for drugs like insulin by up to $7 billion over ten years. *(Source: FTC press release, 2026-02-04.)*
- The **MultiPlan multidistrict litigation (MDL 3121)** is currently progressing through discovery on provider allegations that MultiPlan's algorithms facilitated more than **$19 billion** in single-year out-of-network underpayments, with **Aetna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Cigna, Elevance, and UnitedHealthcare** named as co-defendants. *(Source: MDL 3121 case management order, 2026-02-27.)*
- **Kraft Heinz** has filed an ERISA action against **Aetna** alleging $1.3 million in claims paid without required documentation and refusal to share plan data with the employer. *(Source: Kraft Heinz Foods Co. v. Aetna Life Ins. Co.)*
- The employer-side ERISA cases — including *Lewandowski v. Johnson & Johnson* and *Navarro v. Wells Fargo & Company* — allege that plan sponsors themselves breached fiduciary duty by failing to properly monitor their pharmacy benefit managers. Both were dismissed on standing in early 2026. *(Sources: reported dockets; see also Jones Day, "Rising Scrutiny of Employer Health Plan Administration," May 2026.)*

"These are not internet theories. They are dockets," Flowers said. "The question I am asking is not whether you would put your child in a room with a person whose federal court file looks like that. The question is whether — as a plan fiduciary — you can defend keeping your employees' families in the same room with a *vendor* whose federal court file looks like that."

Flowers cautions HR and finance leaders that they should not read the two employer-side dismissals as vindication.

"The Wells Fargo case was dismissed for standing. The Johnson & Johnson case was dismissed again for standing. HR leaders who take a victory lap on those rulings are reading them wrong," Flowers said. "Those dismissals are the last free pass. The next generation of these cases will be sharper. The plaintiffs will have standing. And the vendor court records are not going to get any cleaner between now and then."

Flowers argues the fix is available now, and — in his professional opinion — is a fiduciary obligation, not a nice-to-have. Superior Insurance Advisors operates on a **client-only compensation model** backed by a **$100,000 Validation Institute credibility guarantee**. The **NextGen Benefits Mastermind Partnership**, of which Flowers is a partner, has for more than a decade published an annual savings benchmark of **at least $2,000 per employee per year** returned to employer balance sheets when plans are moved to a fiduciary-advised structure with independent claims-audit rights.

"Whether you make the change because it saves your CFO millions or because it protects your HR team from the fiduciary-breach case that has not yet been filed against you, is your choice," Flowers said. "The one thing you cannot do — after reading a docket — is later say you didn't know."
