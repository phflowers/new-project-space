# From Four Tags to One Partnership

**Use:** Riverside live conversation + in-person/keynote presentation
**Cover image:** `../images/source/business_cards.jpg`
**Run-time target:** 12–15 min (cuttable to a 5-min pitch; expandable to 30
with the Q&A block)
**Voice:** first-person former insider, per `../voice/style_guide.md`

---

## Beat 1 — Cold open (45–60 sec)

**On screen:** cover photo, all four tags.

> Four tags. Four jobs. One desk. USI. Cigna. Hines. Fountain Health.
> I held every one of these badges. I'm not anonymous. I'm not
> retired. I went into each of these companies looking for a
> partnership. I came out with a story.
>
> Tonight I want to walk you through what I saw, why it changed me,
> and where I landed.

(Hold the photo on screen until every brand is named.)

---

## Beat 2 — Why these four, in this order (60–90 sec)

> I didn't pick these companies randomly. I built into each one. USI.
> Then Cigna. Then Hines. Then Fountain Health. Each one was
> supposed to answer the question the last one raised.
>
> The question never changed: *who is the employer-benefits system
> actually working for?*
>
> I'll tell you up front — I had a hypothesis. The hypothesis was
> "the buyer." The buyer being the CFO, the HR director, the family
> that shows up at open enrollment. By the fourth tag, the data
> wasn't supporting the hypothesis.

## Beat 3 — USI: the consultant chair (90 sec)

**On screen:** `../images/cropped/usi.jpg`

> Vice President. Select Employee Benefits Consultant. Oak Brook,
> Illinois. The title says "consultant." The W-2 says something
> else.
>
> Here's the thing nobody hands you on day one: in this industry,
> "broker" and "consultant" mean different things to the regulator
> and the same thing to the carrier. The compensation stack has
> several layers — base, override, bonus, supplemental. The employer
> sees one. Maybe.
>
> I'm not saying anyone broke a rule. I'm saying the structure
> decides the advice before the advisor opens their mouth.

## Beat 4 — Cigna: the open-enrollment room (90 sec)

**On screen:** `../images/cropped/cigna.jpg`

> Benefit Educator. The badge says it. I stood in front of rooms full
> of employees at open enrollment and walked them through their
> options. PPO. HSA. HDHP. Network. Pharmacy tier.
>
> The script was tight. The questions from the room were not.
>
> "Education" is sold to the employer as a benefit. It's delivered to
> the employee as a funnel. The internal language is "engagement."
> And "steerage." And "leakage." Translate it back: engagement is
> *did they pick the plan we want them to pick.* Leakage is *did they
> go to a doctor we didn't pay for.*
>
> A sick person, in that vocabulary, is a leak.

---

## Beat 5 — Hines: the broker channel (90 sec)

**On screen:** `../images/cropped/hines.jpg`

> National Sales Director, Brokers. National territory.
>
> At USI, I was the broker. At Hines, I was selling *to* brokers.
> Same industry, mirror image. And what you see from that side is
> the distribution layer — who pays whom, who chooses what, on whose
> behalf.
>
> A broker is positioned to an employer as independent. An
> "independent advisor." On paper, often true. But the compensation
> flows that decide which products get recommended don't show up in
> the pitch deck. They show up in the disclosure form the employer
> files a year later.

---

## Beat 6 — Fountain Health: the disruptor pitch (90 sec)

**On screen:** `../images/cropped/fountain_health.jpg`

> Regional Business Development Consultant. The pitch was different —
> a model aimed at doing it better. Preventive-first. Value-based.
> The deck was beautiful.
>
> I have to be honest about my own arc here. I took this role
> because I *wanted* the disruptor pitch to be right. I wanted the
> alternative.
>
> What I learned isn't anyone's secret. It's the gap that every
> "alternative" model has to close: the claims still flow through
> the same rails. The PBM contracts. The network rentals. The
> carrier reinsurance. The deck and the operations meeting are not
> the same meeting.

---

## Beat 7 — The turn (60 sec)

> So that's the four tags. One desk. The moment they all sat on
> that desk at the same time is the moment I stopped selling.
>
> Not because the people inside any of those companies were bad. I
> worked with people I still respect at every single one. The point
> isn't villains. The point is a system that's been optimized for a
> buyer who isn't actually in the room.
>
> The buyer is the employer. The user is the employee. The money
> flows through carriers, brokers, PBMs, and re-insurers. The user
> has no purchase power. The buyer has no real visibility. That
> isn't a bug. That's the design.

---

## Beat 8 — Landing at NextGen Benefits Mastermind (3–4 min)

**On screen:** transition slide — the four tags, plus a fifth: the
NextGen Benefits / Superior Insurance Advisors mark.

> Which brings me to where I am today.
>
> I'm a partner in **NextGen Benefits Mastermind**. I'm CEO of
> **Superior Insurance Advisors**. And in the last few years I
> became one of the first ten advisors in the country — and the
> first African American advisor — to earn the **Fiduciary Advisor

>
> That word "fiduciary" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.
> Let me show you the work.
>
> NextGen Benefits Mastermind is, in the organization's own words,
> the first community built specifically to assemble independent
> benefits advisors who provide better-managed employer health care
> plans — advisors who carry a real fiduciary responsibility to the
> employer-client. Not the carrier. Not the broker network. The
> client.
>
> The way I earned the Validation Institute certification — and the
> way Superior operates day-to-day — is by accepting compensation
> **only from the client.** Not the insurer. Not the vendor. Not
> the PBM. The Validation Institute backs that with a $100,000
> credibility guarantee. If a dollar ever flows to me from the
> other side of your table, I owe it back, and the Institute writes
> you a six-figure check on top. That isn't a marketing line. That
> is the structure.
>
> So when I tell you we put millions back into employers' bottom
> lines, I'm telling you it's structural. It's the by-product of the
> conflict of interest being removed from the chair I sit in.
>
> How does the money actually show up? Through all of the levers
> the system has been hiding from the buyer:
>
> - **Plan structure.** Moving the right employer to a properly
>   designed self-funded or level-funded plan. Sixty-seven percent

>   mid-sized employer is rarely shown the door to it.
> - **Pharmacy.** Pass-through PBM contracts with auditable

> - **Claims-level audit rights.** Not a summary report — the

> - **Plan design and prevention.** Example I run with: lower back

## Live Q&A — anticipated questions and answers

- **"Aren't you just dunking on your former employers?"**
  I'm naming a system I worked inside. If I didn't name the rooms I
  sat in, you'd have no reason to believe a word I'm saying.

- **"What about your NDA / non-compete / non-disparagement
  obligations?"**
  Everything in this talk is from public filings, public marketing,
  my own observation, or labeled clearly as such. Anything covered
  by an agreement, I'm not discussing.

- **"You worked at four companies that overlap. Why should I trust
  the fifth — NextGen Benefits Mastermind — is different?"**
  Fair question, and I'd rather you trust the math than trust me.
  At each of the first four, I was paid by the carrier or the
  vendor — that's how the industry pays. At Superior, under the
  NextGen model, I'm paid only by the client. The Validation
  Institute backs that with a $100,000 guarantee. If you ever find
  a dollar flowing to me from your insurer, your PBM, your
  stop-loss, anyone on the other side of your check — you get your
  money back and the Validation Institute writes you a six-figure
  check on top. The structural conflict is gone. That's why the
  math works.

- **"How much does this cost the employer?"**
  The fee is direct, disclosed, and on a statement of work. No
  "free" advice subsidized by your carrier. I'd rather quote it
  against your specific situation than throw a number that doesn't
  fit. What I will tell you on stage: the published NextGen
  benchmark is at least $2,000 per employee per year back on your
  balance sheet. Run that against your employee count and our fee.
  If the math doesn't work in your favor, I'll tell you.

- **"What's the catch?"**
  Two real ones. First, this is work. It's not a "switch your
  carrier" trick — there's a plan-year window, employee
  communication has to be done right, and the savings show up over
  twelve to twenty-four months, not thirty days. Second, you're
  going to see things in your own data that are hard to look at.
  The savings are real because the waste is real, and the waste was
  yours. That part is uncomfortable. Anyone who tells you otherwise
  is selling you the same pitch you've been getting for ten years.

## Stage notes

- The cover photo is the warrant. Hold it through the cold open
  until every brand is named. Don't cut early.
- Switch to the cropped badge as you start each role beat. Keep it
  up for the whole beat. The image isn't decoration; it's the
  citation of access.
- Short sentences when the fact is heavy. Longer sentences when the
  texture matters (the room, the deck, the script).
- Per `../voice/style_guide.md`: if a claim is from a private
  meeting, say so out loud. ("I can't source this to a filing —
  it's from a conversation I had in [year].")
- Hard lines, on stage same as on the page: no PHI, no named
  clients from any of the four roles, no specific broker partners
  by name, no quoting documents I didn't take with me.

## Cut-down: 5-minute pitch version

If you need the short form (e.g., a podcast intro or a curtain-
raiser for a sales conversation), keep:

- Beat 1 (cold open), full.
- One sentence each from Beats 3, 4, 5, 6 — the most damning line
  from each. Use the cropped images as a rapid montage, ~10 sec each.
- Beat 7 (the turn), full.
- Beat 8 (NextGen Benefits Mastermind), full.
- Beat 9 (close + CTA), full.

That's roughly 4:30–5:00 with clean delivery.
